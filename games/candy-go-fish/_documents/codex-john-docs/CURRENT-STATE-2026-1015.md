## Game and accepted behavior

GO FISH! — Candy Edition! runs in shell slot 8, as plain HTML/CSS/JavaScript without a build. HTTP and direct file opening are supported. The canvas is 9:14, width-relative, touch-controlled and non-scrolling. At John's direction there is no dedicated Pause button. Local dialogs pause presentation; the shell drawer does not notify or pause the game. Score reporting uses only ../game-helper.js.

Ten candy types, three copies each, five dealt to each player, with no completed opening set. Requests must name a held type; success transfers one candy and retains the turn. Triples collect automatically. Fishing always ends the turn. An empty hand, empty dish or all completed sets ends the game after any final set celebration. Most completed sets wins; ties are possible. Four-copy sets are supported internally, but not exposed as a player setting. Good Fish is not implemented.

YES replies hold question/reply for four active seconds before automatic transfer; either middle box or the dish shortcuts the wait. NO replies wait for a middle click to draw. A held requested candy shortcuts an opponent request immediately. Cream questions, green YES, coral NO, curved speaker tails and gentle answer breathing remain. Transfers curve, scale 100%-150%-100% and fade near arrival. Actual candy art flies for public transfers/player draws; opponent private draws remain unidentified. Reduced motion fades.

Persistent piles track physical card IDs and preserve surviving positions. Player hand is the grid; the restored cat tray shows its count. Main dish overflows slightly; opponent tray/dishes are green/red, player dishes brown/yellow. Ceramic pumpkin NEW GAME/RULES controls remain.

## Opening, dialogs and results

The pumpkin-vine welcome keeps the dimmed game artwork behind it. Its action shows Loading assets and is disabled while centralized preload settles, then becomes Let's play. Failed/timed-out assets permit play. The existing startup flash is still unresolved and deferred; this is not a claim that preloading fixes it.

Let's play opens the empty-board starter spinner; a plate or prompt click rotates it for 3.7 seconds, with the existing reduced-motion fallback. The selected character's goes-first speech plays when it lands; a click then deals. Welcome, spinner-start and deal confirmation clicks are silent. confirmText.mp3 is retained for later use.

Game-over score summary is second, ending reason last; the optional remaining-dish line stays between them. The winner portrait/name receives a larger, brighter gold halo than the current-turn highlight. Ties highlight neither side; replay clears the halo.

Original full-screen candy rainfall is the sole game-over celebration for all outcomes. It starts after three seconds: 60% generic bars, 40% branded candies with the original sizes/spin/drift. Both New Game controls stop new drops and let existing drops finish before replay. Early replay cancels delayed rain. The less-glamorous opponent-only spill experiment was rejected and removed, including its test/previews. Do not restore it without a request.

## Preload, soundtrack and channel controls

js/preload.js is the single preparation coordinator: fixed artwork, configured portraits/candies, image loading/decoding, audio loading and spinner-buffer generation. CandySound.prepareAssets supplies 48 short audio elements; prepareWelcomeMusic supplies the three long music elements (all preloaded at John's request; the startup cost on the phone is still to be judged). Cached elements/buffers are reused. Short assets have six-second completion/failure bounds; music has 20 seconds. Music uses a media element plus Web Audio gain, not a fully decoded long PCM buffer. Browser preload is a hint, and the readiness event does not guarantee that every byte is resident on every mobile browser. Preparation does not play audio.

Music appears on welcome, Deal again? and Rules. The chosen tune loops and fades from zero over three seconds once playback is available. Play mode When paused (default): the dialog closing fades it to zero over two seconds and pauses it, preserving currentTime; reopening resumes that position. Play mode Continuous: the dialog closing changes nothing and the tune plays on through the game. Each tune keeps its own position within the page session; a fresh page load starts every tune at zero. Switching tunes fades the old one out over one second and the new one in over three.

Autoplay restrictions still apply. Welcome attempts automatic playback; tapping welcome artwork retries/unlocks audio when required. Let's play enters gameplay on its first click, rather than forcing another click solely to unlock music. Rules/new-round opens originate from user gestures. A failed music load does not leave the action disabled indefinitely.

UI/music have independent gain buses and ON/OFF state. On welcome, Deal again? and Rules the upper-left UI speaker is an on/off toggle and the upper-right MUSIC speaker shows the music state (slash = off) and opens the Music popup. The popup is a second overlay sized like the Rules box so it fully covers the dialog beneath: Music ON/OFF, Soundtrack with arrows around the tune name, Play mode switch (When paused / Continuous), Volume slider with stops 0, 20, 40, 60, 80, 100, then Cancel (bottom left, restores what was in force when it opened) and Done (bottom right, keeps and saves). Changes apply immediately. The music channel is scaled by 1/0.75, so the original level sits at slider 75 and the default is 80. Settings (enabled, volume, track, mode, ui) are saved to localStorage under halloween2026:candy-go-fish:music on Done and on the UI toggle, restored at startup with every value checked; requirements rule 21 was relaxed on October 15 to allow this. Touch targets are at least 13vw; Tab cycles inside the popup. UI mute stops effects/pending victory without stopping music. Music OFF pauses immediately, preserving position; ON resumes with fade-in. Earned-set dialogs do not show these controls. Per-sound defaults are 50%. Device volume remains user-controlled. The shell Sound switch has no game API and still does not control this game.

## October 16 (Claude, at John's direction): sounds from decoded buffers

- **The problem.** On John's iPhone (live site, in the shell) every sound that went through a media element was unreliable: the goes-first voice and the victory speech (timer-started) were silent, the character arrows (tap-started) were silent too, the music was a coin flip on the artwork tap, and iOS Now Playing showed the tune as "paused" when play was asked before the file was ready. Only the spinner ticks, played from a decoded buffer, always worked. Diagnosed with the ?debug=sound panel on October 15, widened by John's tests on the morning of the 16th; plan and options in claude-john-docs/claudePlans/iphone-speech-buffers-plan.md. John chose Option C after a fallback tag (before-iphone-speech-buffers = 300675c) was pushed.
- **Option C, built.** sound.js: the twelve short sounds (confirm, two fanfares, three arrows, six goes-first) are fetched and decoded into AudioBuffers at startup (prepareAssets returns [{ url, ready }] so preload.js can wait and record failures within the six-second bound); play(sound) starts an AudioBufferSourceNode through a per-play gain into the UI channel and returns a voice ({ sound, duration, gain, onended }); stopAll('ui') stops every voice at once (the 84-idle-clips problem is gone for good). The 36 victory speeches and the tie decode on demand: scheduleVictory calls prepareSpeech at once and plays when both the two-second timer and the decode are done (a cancel during the decode is honoured through a token); the buffer is cached for the replay button; the game-over music timing uses the voice's duration and onended as before. A failed fetch or decode is forgotten so the next play() retries. The music stays as three media elements.
- **Two music rules.** Nothing starts the music without a tap: the loading-finished auto-start is gone, and the welcome artwork listens for click as well as pointerdown (unlock + startWelcomeMusic). Every click on the page calls CandySound.userTapped() (wake the engine, then retryMusic: restart the current tune when it is wanted and not playing). The listener is on document, so it runs after the tapped element's own handler; a tap that fades the music out in Paused mode is not undone.
- **Limits.** Decoded sounds need HTTP: Chrome refuses fetch() over file://, so a page opened from disk has music but no UI sounds or speech (file:// was only ever a developer convenience). The game-over music in Paused mode is still asked for from a timer, so on the phone it may wait for the next tap.
