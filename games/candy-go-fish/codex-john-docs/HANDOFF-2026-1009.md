## Game summary

One human plays against a computer opponent, initially named Wendy. Players request candies they already hold and collect complete sets. The base game uses ten candy types and three copies of each: thirty physical candies, ten possible completed sets. A completed set contains three identical candies and leaves the active hand. Both players' completed sets are public; active hands are private.

The human selects a candy slot, sees that candy in the center, then presses **Got Any?**. A successful request receives exactly ONE candy and allows another request. A failed request leads to a single go-fish draw, which ALWAYS ends the turn, even if it matches the requested candy or completes a set.

The interface is deliberately asymmetric: the human sees their own quantities in fixed candy slots, while Wendy's slots reveal only completed sets. Wendy's algorithm must be treated as unable to see the human's displayed quantities.

## Status of decisions

### Agreed baseline

- Ten types; three copies per type; collect triples.
- A request is legal only for a type currently held in the requesting player's active hand. Completed sets do not qualify.
- Transfer one candy per successful request, even if the other player has two.
- A successful request continues the requester's turn. They can request the same type again or choose another legal type.
- Every go-fish draw ends the turn, regardless of what was drawn.
- Human requests use selection followed by Got Any?, rather than immediate commitment on tapping a slot.
- The human clicks the central pile to take their go-fish draw.
- Fixed slots identify the ten candy types; completed sets gain highlighting and a green checkmark.
- Wendy's active hand is hidden; her total hand count is visible.
- Turn appearance uses gentle emphasis of the active half, a persistent turn label, and emphasis of the available controls.
- Empty-slot candy visibility must be adjustable through a 0–100 transparency variable; 100 makes the image disappear while leaving the slot.
- No timed moves or time penalties. No dedicated Pause button is currently intended.

### Working proposals, not final approvals

- Five candies initially dealt to each player, with twenty in the central pile, from the earlier proposed rules.
- Random starting player: John suggested this as probable, rather than settling it definitively.
- One point per completed set; display both totals and report the human's set total to the shell. This was proposed in discussion and should be reviewed during implementation.
- Stacked candy images plus quantity badges for occupied human slots. John accepted trying the faint image for the empty slot; the occupied-slot treatment was not separately confirmed.
- Refill and depleted-pile behavior below retain earlier proposals and need confirmation.
- Wendy's fishing draw uses an unidentified visual to preserve privacy; this was suggested, not explicitly approved.

## Rules and completion

1. On a turn, request a type held in the active hand.
2. If the opponent holds that type, transfer one copy. Collect a resulting triple automatically, and continue the requester's turn.
3. If the opponent has none, draw one from the unused pile when available. Collect a resulting triple automatically, and pass the turn.
4. A fishing draw never grants another turn. This explicitly replaces the original matching-draw extra-turn rule.
5. Completed sets remain visible but cannot be requested or transferred.
6. End immediately when all ten sets have been collected across the two boards. Neither board needs to fill all ten slots individually.
7. With one point per set, the higher total wins; 5–5 is a valid tie in the base game.

### Empty hands and depleted pile — retained proposal

The earlier rules refill an empty hand with up to five candies, collect any triples, and repeat if still empty and cards remain. Refill the acting player before the other player. Refilling itself does not grant a turn; the request outcome determines the next player. An empty player with no pile skips their turn.

When the pile is empty, a failed request passes the turn without a draw. Do not leave the interface waiting for a click on an empty pile. Successful requests still transfer one candy and continue the turn.

These details have not been reapproved during the October 9 discussion. They must be resolved before treating the rules as final. Once the pile is empty, any uncompleted type must be split between the hands, because complete triples are removed immediately. A legal request therefore makes progress in a valid state. One-copy transfers may require another request to complete the set.

Every slot has a stable candy identity and an active-hand quantity. Empty slots cannot be requested. Completed slots remain visible and cannot be selected for requests.

Proposed appearance: faint identity image for zero, one image for one, two modestly offset images for two, and three stacked images plus highlight/checkmark at completion. Use explicit number badges for active quantities rather than requiring counting. At completion the checkmark can replace the quantity badge.

Expose `emptyCandyTransparency` as a simple design variable ranging from 0 to 100: 0 = fully visible; 100 = invisible. Convert to CSS opacity with `1 - transparency / 100`. This controls the empty identity image, not the whole slot or its interaction state.

### Wendy slot appearance

Uncompleted slots do not reveal whether she holds zero, one, or two copies. A completed type displays its candy with a green checkmark and highlighting. The holding count counts only active candies, excluding completed sets. If she receives a candy and completes a triple, update her total for both the receipt and removal of three candies.

## Gameplay interaction flow

### Human turn

1. Emphasize the human half and display YOUR TURN. Prompt: Choose a candy.
2. Tap an eligible slot. Show its candy in the center and emphasize Got Any?. Allow changing selection before committing.
3. Press Got Any? once. Lock further requests while resolving and presenting the outcome.
4. If Wendy has the type, announce a short response, animate ONE matching candy from her private-hand area to the human slot, update quantities, then show any completed set.
5. Return to human selection because the request succeeded. The player freely chooses the next legal type.
6. If Wendy has none, show a short No… go fish response and replace the center request image with the pile. Prompt: Tap the pile to go fish.
7. Wait for the human's tap. Animate the drawn candy from the center to its fixed human slot, update the quantity and any completion, then change to Wendy's turn.

### Wendy turn

1. Emphasize Wendy's half and display WENDY'S TURN. Use a brief readable delay before her request.
2. Wendy chooses a legal type using only allowed information, displays it in the center, and asks Got any?
3. If the human holds that candy, ONE candy moves from its human slot to Wendy's private-hand area. The described flow makes this transfer automatic; no separate Give button has been agreed.
4. Update both active-hand counts and show any new Wendy set. Wendy continues her turn and can request the same or another held type.
5. If the human holds none, prompt them to click Go Fish. The player cannot falsely refuse a candy they hold.
6. After that click, show the central pile, animate Wendy's draw to her private-hand area, update her count and any completed set, then give the human the turn.

The actual candy in Wendy's fishing animation is unresolved. John's description says a candy icon glides to her pile. Revealing its identity would make that draw public; an unidentified token would preserve her hand privacy. Choose explicitly before implementation, and ensure the public event model matches the choice.

Check game completion after each logical resolution. Do not wait for another input once the final set is collected. Finish presenting the decisive transfer/completion, then show win, loss, or tie, totals, and Play again.

## Turn emphasis and feedback

Use a subtle warm brightening behind the active player's half, keeping the inactive half readable. Maintain a persistent YOUR TURN or WENDY'S TURN label beside the corresponding player icon. On turn change, gently fade emphasis between halves and briefly enlarge the incoming label. No continuous flashing.

Emphasize only presently available controls: eligible human slots, Got Any? after selection, the pile when drawing, or Go Fish when responding to Wendy. Put brief phase instructions near the center. A successful request does not trigger a false turn-change animation.

Use short readable outcome text, explicit counts, and checkmarks; color and motion must not be the only signals. Preserve keyboard activation and visible focus as useful additions while remaining fully touch playable. Announce meaningful results through a polite live region.

## Candy transfer animation

The objective is to look like a physical candy being handed over, rather than a count changing in place.

- Animate a copy of the actual candy image from its source to its destination, preserving proportions.
- Try a slight arc, gentle acceleration/deceleration, and modest rotation. Keep timing configurable and test it at phone size.
- Human receipt: Wendy's private-hand area → correct human slot.
- Human gift: correct human slot → Wendy's private-hand area.
- Human draw: central pile → correct human slot.
- Wendy draw: central pile → Wendy's private-hand area, using the agreed privacy treatment.
- Present receipt, quantity update, and completed-set highlight/checkmark in a readable sequence.
- Never remove or flicker the fixed slot identity simply because an animated copy is traveling.
- Disable duplicate input while resolving. Animation completion must not run the same move twice.
- Respect reduced motion by shortening or replacing travel with a simple transition while preserving the information.

The engine resolves a move exactly once; presentation displays resulting events. Animation callbacks must not determine whether a request succeeds or whose turn comes next. Resizing and closing overlays must not corrupt state.

## Wendy's strategy and information boundary

Wendy must receive her own hand, public counts, completed sets, public requests/transfers, and whatever draw information the final interface intentionally reveals. Do not pass her the human hand, human slot quantities, or unused-pile order. The engine can inspect both hands to enforce rules; the strategy cannot.

A request inevitably reveals that Wendy holds at least one of that candy. This is inherent in the legal-request rule; hiding her other types is the achievable goal.

Suggested starting strategy:

1. Favor types where public evidence indicates the human holds a copy.
2. Give extra value to completing a pair, while recognizing that holding one leaves two copies elsewhere and may offer a better chance of finding a match.
3. After receiving one candy, update evidence: the human may still have another, but a successful one-copy transfer does not prove that.
4. A failed request proves absence at that moment. Weaken or clear absence evidence when the human fishes or refills, because the hand may have changed.
5. A human request proves possession at that moment. Update that belief after transfers and remove it when the human completes that set.
6. Eliminate completed types from all candidates and memory. Choose only currently held, uncompleted types.
7. Randomize among reasonable candidates, so Wendy does not mechanically request every pair or repeat one pattern.

This can be a small weighted heuristic with understandable named functions. It does not need an elaborate optimal solver. Review its behavior through simulated games and actual play. Do not claim fairness merely because a random choice is involved; enforce the information boundary structurally.

## Scoring and future variations

Proposed base score: one point per completed set. Display both players' totals; report the human total at game end through the shared helper. A possible short display text is the final score, e.g. `6–4`, ranked by the human total. Exact score display/reporting remains to be confirmed. The helper does not save zero or negative scores.

Potential future binary variations discussed:

- Ten vs. eleven candy types. Eleven total sets would eliminate ties under one-point-per-set scoring, but requires another candy asset and another slot per board.
- Matching three vs. four. This requires four physical copies per type, different count/stack presentation, and strategy and pacing review.

Keep type count and copies per type configurable internally, but implement the base game first. Do not introduce these switches as functioning scaffold controls before the helper supports reading them. If scores from different configurations are later mixed, decide how to compare them; raw set totals have different maxima.

## Halloween 2026 scaffold integration

The shell manages a collection of games. Its outer frame is 9:16; each game owns only the 9:14 iframe panel. The shell already provides a top bar containing Home, the current game's short title, and the menu control. Its bottom drawer contains game browsing and a This Game tab with metadata, instructions, best scores, Sound, and declared switches.

### Normal integration

- Build `games/candy-go-fish/index.html` with local CSS, JavaScript, and assets.
- Include `../game-helper.js` before the game's own script if reporting scores.
- Add a `games.json` entry with folder, title/short title, available menu slot, icon, credits, description, and how-to-play text. Candy Go Fish is not currently registered. Slot choice is open; do not silently displace a game.
- Supply a square icon following registry specifications: normally 256 × 256 transparent PNG, or square-viewBox SVG.
- Keep the game independently runnable. Avoid relying on fetch-only setup if it prevents direct local opening.
- Ordinary registration does not require changes in `app/`.
- Use only the helper to communicate with the shell. No direct `window.parent`, `postMessage`, cookies, or localStorage access from game code.
- Before publishing code changes, follow the repository's version-stamp convention: `APP_VERSION` in `app/app.js` and both `?v=` stamps in root `index.html`.

### Internal top strip — revised proposal

The earlier suggestion of Exit / score / Rules overlooked the shell's existing navigation and title. Prefer a compact in-game strip with score and Rules, reserving the shell's Home for navigation. No internal Pause button is currently intended. An internal Exit could mean returning to the game's own start screen, but must not pretend it can navigate the shell through an unsupported API.

The shell's Home or selecting another game immediately unloads the iframe and discards the game. There is no exit-confirmation or save/resume handshake. Adding shell-level confirmation would require an explicit helper/app API design and coordinated shell work; it is not required for the base game.

### Pause requirement / deliberate exception to resolve

John observed that a turn-based game has no timed move requirements and can simply wait while the drawer is open. The agreed direction is to omit a dedicated Pause button. Wendy might finish an animation, ask another question, or make successive successful requests before reaching an input wait, but the human suffers no timeout or time penalty.

The current binding requirement 18 nevertheless says any game with automatic movement must provide pause/resume. Our intended design includes both automatic Wendy actions and candy animations, so this is a real requirements exception, not a claim that the rule already permits it. Before release, document the rationale in the game README's **Where this game breaks the requirements, and why** section, using the repository's existing convention, or have the owner revise the shared requirement to cover turn-based games without timed penalties. No shell drawer notification or automatic pause integration is currently requested.

Rules overlays should suspend pending Wendy presentation and animation sequencing while open, then resume safely. This is a local overlay behavior, not detection of the shell drawer. Ensure Wendy cannot enter an endless unattended sequence; legal finite transfers naturally limit successful requests, and a failed request waits for the human's Go Fish response.

### Switches and sound — future scaffold work

The current shell supports up to THREE game switches, although John discussed two useful variations. It renders and stores them but games cannot read them. `Halloween.reportScore` is the helper's only current gameplay call. Sound similarly has no game-facing API.

To activate the proposed variations through the shell, future coordinated work must define and implement a helper settings-read API, expose the selected settings from the app, and update the shared requirements and examples. Proposed behavior from historical shell discussions is to apply game settings to the next newly started game; this remains unimplemented and should be confirmed. Do not invent a private bridge from Candy Go Fish.

If audio is later added, agree a supported global Sound API first; historical discussion requires Sound OFF to silence already-playing audio immediately. Audio is not necessary to this base prototype.

## Layout and implementation requirements

Follow the shared page setup, width-relative sizing, no scrolling, touch settings, and Arial/Helvetica/sans-serif typography. Minimum interactive size is 13vw in BOTH directions (168 × 168 on the design canvas); minimum text is 3.6vw with the specified pixel floors. Maintain about 2vw target gaps and keep important targets out of the bottom 5vw.

The mockup's 168 × 168 squares meet the minimum if preserved at scale. Its separate 168 × 84 examples cannot serve as interactive targets at that height. A thin in-game information strip must still give Rules a full-height compliant hit area. Do not reuse the earlier October 8 proposal to scroll the hand; the fixed board must fit without scrolling.

Suggested code separation remains: pure rules engine, restricted-information computer strategy, rendering, and presentation sequencing. Use small named functions and comments explaining choices, understandable to an intermediate JavaScript programmer. Track distinct phases such as human selection, human confirmation, transfer presentation, human fishing wait, Wendy thinking/request, human Go Fish response, Wendy draw presentation, rules overlay, and finished.

Core invariants:

- Every physical candy exists exactly once across pile, hands, and sets.
- A completed set contains all three copies of one type; no active hand retains a complete triple after resolution.
- Every accepted request is for a type held by its actor.
- A successful request transfers exactly one copy and retains the turn.
- A fishing outcome passes the turn regardless of the drawn type.
- Completed types have one owner and cannot return to active play.
- Wendy strategy cannot read hidden human quantities or pile order.
- Finished play has ten completed sets and accepts no further moves.
